## Biography

Michael Tsalka is currently serving as an Assistant Professor and Doctoral Supervisor at the School of Music, The Chinese University of Hong Kong in Shenzhen. As a concert pianist and early keyboard performer, he has won numerous prizes in Europe, Asia, North America, and Latin America. He is a versatile musician, who performs repertoire from the Renaissance to our days. He has released fifty critically acclaimed CDs, which include many world-premiere recordings, for labels such as NAXOS, Grand Piano, Paladino, Brilliant Classics, IMI, Sheva Collection, Wirripang, Tempus Clasico, Rattle, Da Vinci Classics, and Ljud & Bild. Together with musicologist Dr. Angélica Minero Escobar, he prepared a critical edition of Daniel Gottlob Türk's 30 keyboard sonatas for Artaria Editions in New Zealand. Additionally, his scholarly articles have been published in music journals in Italy, the U.S.A., and the Netherlands. Prof. Tsalka maintains a busy concert schedule, performing circa 80 concerts a year. Recent engagements include the Hall of Central Harmony (Beijing, Forbidden City), Bellas Artes Theater (Mexico City), the Hermitage Museum (St. Petersburg), the Metropolitan Museum (New York), St. Denis Festival (Paris), Beethoven House (Bonn), City Opera (Tokyo), the National Gallery of Art (Washington, D.C.), Volksbühne (Berlin), the Jerusalem Music Centre, Kazan Conservatory of Music, Museo de la Música (Barcelona), the Mozarteum (Salzburg), Concertgebouw (Amsterdam), the ElbPhilharmonie (Hamburg), Shanghai Concert Hall, Xinghai Concert Hall (Guangzhou), plus live performances for radio and television stations around the globe. He has performed as soloist with many orchestras, including the Sydney Consort and Thorough Bass (Australia), Musica Florea (Prague, Czech Republic), Xiamen Philharmonic Orchestra, Birmingham University Chamber Orchestra (U.K.), and the Krasnoyarsk Chamber Orchestra (Russia). Dr. Tsalka was born in Tel Aviv, Israel. After studying in Israel, Germany, and Italy, he graduated in 2008 from Temple University (U.S.A) with a D.M.A. in Piano Performance and two Master's degrees in Early Keyboard Performance and in Chamber Music. His mentors included Dina Turjeman, Lambert Orkis, Joyce Lindorff, and Harvey Wedeen, as well as Dario di Rosa, Klaus Schilde, Malcolm Bilson, David Shemer, Sandra Mangsen, and Charles Rosen. From 2009 to 2020, Prof. Tsalka was the artistic director of Music Festivals in the Netherlands, China, Finland, Sweden, and Spain. From 2009 to 2014, he was a professor of harpsichord and chamber music at the Escuela Superior de Música, National Center for the Arts in Mexico City, and at Lilla Akademien, Stockholm. Apart from his current position at CUHK-Shenzhen, he is a member of the prestigious European Academy of Sciences and Arts (EASA), Salzburg, Austria, the Chairman of the Board of the European Fortepiano Museum and Academy (EFM) in Germany, a visiting Professor at Celaya Conservatory of Music in Guanajuato, Mexico, and an Artist-in-Residence at the Nelson Center of Musical Arts (NCMA, New Zealand). He has taught over 190 master classes in institutions from all continents. 2025 highlights include performances at the Kunsthistorisches Museum (Vienna); the modern world premiere performance of Vanhal's Clavier Concerto in A Major, alongside the orchestral ensemble Musica Florea at the Liechtenstein Palace in Prague; the world premiere performance of Nicholas Smith's Allegro for Piano and a Wind Band in Chengdu, China; plus solo and chamber music performances at the Muenster Theater (Germany), the Tel-Aviv Museum, Fitzwilliam College (Cambridge), and the Divertimento International Music Festival (Mexico City). Prof. Tsalka also presented master classes and lecture-recitals at the Paderewski Academy of Music in Poznan, at Auckland University, and the Schubert Society (Sydney). 2026 highlights include Forest of Dreams, a four-hand Schubert piano program (and conversations) recorded live in Tokyo alongside fortepianist Megumi Tanno, played on an 1830 Rosenberger original fortepiano. These videos, recently released on YouTube, have been watched more than 300.000 times. Dr. Tsalka has also delivered performances, lectures, and masterclasses at Musikhochschulen in Austria and Germany: Karlsruhe, Eisenstadt, and Oldenburg; taught and performed at the Tyreso Palace Chamber Music Festival in Stockholm and at the Euro Arts Festival in Szczecin, Poland. He also performed at the inaugural concert presenting the Mietke harpsichord and the Rieger Baroque organ at CUHK-Shenzhen. Fall 2026 activities include a CD recording for the Chandos label, performing and teaching at The Days of New Music Festival in Moldova; judging, performing, and lecturing at the finals of the Beethoven International Youth Competition in Shanghai; and performances in Chengdu and Shantou. Prof. Tsalka has collaborated with musicians such as Lambert Orkis, Cynthia Roberts, Peter Sykes, Alon Sariel, Bridget Douglas, and Christopher Hogwood. His chamber music performances often include complete cycles, among them: J. S. Bach's Viola da Gamba and Harpsichord Sonatas, W. A. Mozart's Clavier and Violin Sonatas, as well as his complete works for four hands, L. v. Beethoven's Clavier and Cello Sonatas, F. Schubert's Four Hands Clavier works, and Brahms's Cello and Piano and Violin and Piano Sonatas. Dr. Tsalka often collaborates with contemporary composers and premieres their works: circa 90 compositions from all across the globe were dedicated to him. Prof. Tsalka's students have been accepted to prestigious institutions worldwide, often with full grants and awards, and have won first prizes in International Competitions. Recent performances of his students included the Euro Arts Festival in Szczecin, Poland; in Hong Kong SAR; at the Grand Theatre of the Guangming Culture and Arts Center in Shenzhen; at the Macao Piano Competition Finals; and at Shanghai Conservatory. In 2025, Prof. Tsalka won the CUHK-Shenzhen Ethics Teaching Award.
