## Biography

Prof. Kim, Sun Jin graduated from The Ohio State University in 2011 with a Bachelor of Science in Pharmaceutical Sciences. Then, Prof. Kim earned her Ph.D. in Pharmaceutics and Pharmaceutical Chemistry at the University of Utah, followed by postdoctoral researcher training also at the University of Utah in the lab of Professor Shawn C. Owen. During her Ph.D. and postdoctoral studies, she worked on engineering split-luciferase to develop assays for detecting and quantifying different forms of cancer proteins and their interactions. Later, she expanded the applications of split-luciferase technology to monitor antibody levels in clinical samples and to measure immunity. After finishing her postdoctoral training, she worked as a Staff Research Associate III in the Department of Medicine at the University of California, San Francisco (UCSF). At UCSF, she studied HIV, particularly the effects of substances or combinations of therapies on altering HIV transcription levels or inducing post-treatment controls using various transcriptomics and single-cell sequencing technologies. Her past research experiences have drawn her attention to applying split-protein systems in designing and developing new diagnostics and omics assays. Her future research interests will focus on engineering split-protein systems to utilize them as high-throughput detection and quantification tools that can characterize biomolecules involved in cell biology concerning pathogenic pathways and mechanisms.
