## Biography

Professor Ackerman is a Clinical Professor, Board Certified Family Physician with over 40 years of clinical practice, teaching, and leadership in faculty, course, and curricular development. She is an expert in clinical teaching in both undergraduate and graduate (family medicine) medical education and has been a founding faculty for new medical schools and residencies in the US and Asia. Most recently she was at the forefront of developing two new medical schools in Texas, Burnett School of Medicine and Tilman J Fertitta Family College of Medicine in Clinical Skills, Communication, Phase 3 Clerkships and Electives, and Global Health. She graduated from Texas A&M University with a Bachelor of Science in Biology and obtained her Doctor of Medicine (MD) from Texas A&M Health Sciences College of Medicine, with honors. She completed her Family Medicine Residency at John Peter Smith Family Medicine Residency in Fort Worth, Texas, where she led their family centered obstetrics, didactics, and global health programs which later became fellowships. While leading the Alaska Family Medicine Residency Program (University of Washington) she completed the National Institute of Program Directors Fellowship (NIPDD) through the American Academy of Family Physicians. She has held numerous academic and clinical leadership positions throughout her career, including Professor and Director of Clinical Skills at the Burnett School of Medicine in Fort Worth, Chair of Family Medicine at United Family Health in Guangzhou, China, Program Director of the Family Medicine Residency across Guangzhou, Beijing, and Shanghai, Associate Professor and Family Medicine Program Director at Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center at the Permian Basin, and Director of Academics at the Alaska Family Medicine Residency Program. Her international experience includes serving as Professor and founding faculty at Kathmandu University Medical School in Nepal, where she helped start a new medical school with a problem-based learning curriculum, and as Program Director and Consultant for the Post-Graduate Generalist Program Development in the Kingdom of Bhutan. She is the co-founder and Medical Director of the Health and Environmental Learning Program (H.E.L.P.), a non-profit community development organization based in the Himalayas, and founder of Jyoti Niwas (House of Light) orphanage and Jyoti Jiwan (Light of Life) community-based program for disadvantaged children in Nepal. She has been recognized with numerous awards including the Fulbright Specialist Roster (2024), Service Award from the US Consulate Guangzhou (2019), Faculty of the Year at Alaska Family Medicine Residency Program (2014) and John Peter Smith Family Medicine Residency Program, and Texas "Super Doc" by Texas Monthly (2009). She has published over 30 book chapters and multiple peer reviewed articles and is a popular speaker and presenter on multiple topics nationally and internationally.
