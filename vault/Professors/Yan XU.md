## Biography

Yan Xu specializes in comparative and international political economy, with focuses on China, technology and innovation, global supply chains, and state-business relations. His book manuscript examines how the Chinese state tries to harness the “Silicon Valley Model” of startups and venture capital to develop high-tech industries. Whereas active entrepreneurship is often associated with market-oriented financial systems, he shows China has combined state intervention in finance with active startups. The project further studies how increasing entrepreneurship has affected China’s development in knowledge-intensive industries such as semiconductors and biopharma as a latecomer. In addition, he has researched the relations between the state and top business tycoons in China and the politics of the global semiconductor industry. His research has appeared in Politics & Society and Socio-Economic Review and has been supported by the Charles Koch Foundation, and the Center for East Asian Studies and the George J. Stigler Center for the Study of the Economy and the State at the University of Chicago. He received a Ph.D. in Political Science from the University of Chicago and an M.A. and B.A. from Tsinghua University.
