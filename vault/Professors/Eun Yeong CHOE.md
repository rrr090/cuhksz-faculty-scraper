## Biography

Dr. Eun Yeong Choe is an Assistant Professor at the School of Public Policy, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen. She holds a PhD in Landscape Architecture from the University of Sheffield, where her doctoral research investigated how urban natural environments can support health care delivery and contribute to evidence-based public policy. Dr. Choe's research examines how diverse individuals experience, engage with, and perceive their surroundings, with a focus on informing holistic and socially innovative urban policy. She is committed to bridging urban design, public health, and community well-being, generating research that supports healthier, more equitable, and socially cohesive cities. Her work also examines practitioners' roles in facilitating community participation and creative methods for inclusive policy engagement. She founded the Health, Environment, and Social Equity (HESE) Lab, which advances evidence-based solutions at the intersection of public health, environmental justice, and social equity. The research team integrates scientific inquiry with strategic policy design, translating analytical findings into scalable, high-impact policy solutions. Learn more at the HESE Policy Lab website: https://spp-heselab.cuhk.edu.cn/ Following her PhD, Dr. Choe served as lead author of the EU Interreg North Sea Region policy report Designing Blue Green Infrastructure for Water Management, Human Health, and Wellbeing. She then joined the Department of Urban Planning and Design at the University of Hong Kong as a postdoctoral fellow, collaborating with interdisciplinary teams, community stakeholders, and public sector partners to translate research into policy outcomes for healthy urban redevelopment. She previously served as a Research Assistant Professor at the School of Design, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University (2023–2025), where she fostered an inclusive learning environment and mentored students in evidence-based approaches to urban policy. Her research-driven teaching equips students to critically analyze urban issues and contribute to policy discourse.
