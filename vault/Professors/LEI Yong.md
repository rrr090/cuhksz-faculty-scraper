## Biography

Dr. Yong Lei received his Bachelors’ degree from Wuhan University. He earned his Ph.D. in Biomedical Sciences at The Chinese University of Hong Kong in 2013, carrying out genome editing strategies (ZFNs, TALENs, CRISPR) to construct animal models in studying early embryogenesis. As a pioneer researcher achieving targeted genome editing in vivo, his paper was cited by Science as Ten Breakthroughs of 2012. He then joined the lab of renowned hematologist Prof. Margaret A. Goodell at Baylor College of Medicine (BCM) for postdoc training, focusing on DNA hypermethylation editing and the role of aberrant DNA methylation in leukemogenesis. Applying the state-of-the-art methylation editing technique, he published the first study on targeted hypermethylation in mice zygote worldwide. In June 2020, he The Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen) as an Assistant Professor. His research interests include deterioration of epigenome proof-reading system in ageing and its role in re-sculpturing epigenetic marks, and the maintenance of induced DNA hypermethylation through intergenerational inheritance. He has published multiple papers in high-impact journals, including Nature Genetics, Genome Biology, Nature Communications, Nucleic Acids Research and PNAS.
