## Biography

ZHANG Jun recently joined the Chinese University of Hong Kong at Shenzhen as a fixed-term Professor in the School of Artificial Intelligence. He has been a tenured Full Professor of Psychology and Full Professor of Statistics at the University of Michigan Ann Arbor. He is a fellow of Association of Psychological Sciences and a fellow of Psychonomic Society. He is a founding Co-Editor of the journal Information Geometry and served as an Associate Editor of the Journal of Mathematical Psychology. He has served as the President, Vice President, and member of Executive Board of the Society for Mathematical Psychology, and as a Council Member and then elected as a Member-at-Large on the Governing Board of the Federation of Associations in Brain and Behavioral Sciences (FABBS). Prof. ZHANG obtained B.S. degree in Theoretical Physics at Fudan University in 1985, and Ph.D. degree in Neurobiology at the University of California at Berkeley in 1992, when he was then hired as a tenure-track and then fully tenured faculty at the University of Michigan (currently on leave). During sabbatical years, he held visiting positions at the University of Melbourne (Australia), CNRS Marseille (France), University of Waterloo (Canada), RIKEN Brain Science Institute (Japan), Newton Institute of Cambridge University (UK), Center for Mathematical Sciences and Applications (CMSA) at Harvard University, etc. He conducts research in computation cognitive modeling, machine learning, artificial intelligence and brain-like computation through the M3 (“Mind, Machine and Mathematics”) Lab he directs. In the last two decades, Prof. ZHANG focuses on Information Geometry, which is the differential geometric characterization of the manifold of probability density functions and the duality of information spaces, and advances the notion of “Statistical Mirror Symmetry” in an effort to generalize Shannon entropy in classic information coding theory and exponential family of probability distributions in statistics. A potentially fruitful direction of application to neural computation is unified brain model based on free-energy and other concepts in non-equilibrium statistical physics. Additionally, Professor ZHANG is interested in computation psychiatry and the cognitive and emotional processes implicated in various mental illness (mood disorder and psychosis).
