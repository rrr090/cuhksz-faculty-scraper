## Biography

XU Jinchao is a Professor at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen (CUHK-Shenzhen) ， Director of the Center for Artificial Intelligence for Science and Engineering (AI4SE) at Shenzhen Loop Area Institute, and Professor of Applied Mathematics and Computational Sciences at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST). He was formerly Verne M. Willaman Professor of Mathematics at The Pennsylvania State University, Director of the Penn State–Peking University Joint Center for Computational Mathematics and Applications, Changjiang Chair Professor at Peking University, and a recipient of China’s Thousand Talents Program at Peking University . His primary research interests include the design, analysis, and application of numerical methods for scientific computing—particularly finite element and multigrid methods—as well as machine learning, including deep neural networks and large language models. His representative contributions include pioneering theory and algorithms in multigrid and domain decomposition methods; the development of the FASP software package; the formulation of the subspace correction framework; and foundational work on the mathematics of deep learning. He has also contributed to machine learning through MgNet, which bridges ideas from numerical analysis and convolutional neural networks, and AceGPT, an LLM developed specifically for Arabic. Several influential theories and algorithms are named after him (“X”), including the BPX preconditioner, HX preconditioner, XZ identity, and the MWX element. He received the inaugural Feng Kang Prize in Scientific Computing in 1995, the Humboldt Research Award for Senior Scientists in 2005, and the National Science Fund for Distinguished Young Scholars (Category B) in 2006. He was an invited speaker at the Sixth International Congress on Industrial and Applied Mathematics (ICIAM 2007) and delivered a 45-minute invited lecture at the International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM 2010). He is a Fellow of the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (SIAM, 2011), the American Mathematical Society (AMS, 2012), and the American Association for the Advancement of Science (AAAS, 2019). He was elected a member of the European Academy of Sciences (EURASC) in 2022 and Academia Europaea in 2023.
